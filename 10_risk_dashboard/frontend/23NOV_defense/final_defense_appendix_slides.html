Appendix A1: Formal Feature Attribution (FFA) — Best-Model CNF & AXP Bounds

Specific Aim 2 · Steps 6–8 & 10 Mathematical Verification & PySAT AXP

Step 7 Dual-Model Gate & Step 8 CNF Compilation

  • Step 7 Dual-Model TreeSHAP Gate: \[\mathcal{F}_{\text{SHAP}} = \text{supp}(\text{SHAP}_{\text{CatBoost}}) \cap \text{supp}(\text{SHAP}_{\text{BestXGB}})\] Requires cross-architecture agreement before symbolic extraction (since CatBoost CTR hashing precludes direct symbolic path parsing).
  • Step 8 CNF Compilation on best_xgb_variant:
    • Parses JSON tree dumps of best_xgb_variant (xgb vs. xgb_rf winner by PR-AUC then Recall).
    • Explodes root-to-leaf paths into tabular intervals (_explode_tree_to_dataframe) and compiles Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF) clauses (rule_clauses).

SHAP-Guided Minimal Hitting-Set AXP & Complexity

  • SHAP-Guided Rule Filter \(\rightarrow\) PySAT Hitman:
    • Rather than brute-forcing \(>10,000\) matched tree rules per patient, prioritizes top SHAP-supported matched rules (with stratified rare-population coverage safeguards) into the PySAT Hitman minimal hitting-set solver to extract minimal Abductive Explanations (AXPs / Horn clauses) with \(\text{Supp}(R) \ge 0.05\) and \(\text{Conf}(R) \ge 0.70\).
  • Step 8 Attribution Responsibility & Step 10 Combined Synthesis: \[\text{AttrResp}(f) = \frac{\text{RuleFreq}(f)}{\sum_k \text{RuleFreq}(f_k)} \times \text{SHAP}_{\text{consensus}}(f)\] \[\text{Score}_{\text{combined}}(f) = 0.5 \cdot \text{SHAP}_{\text{norm}}(f) + 0.5 \cdot \text{FFA}_{\text{norm}}(f)\]
  • Complexity vs. Neural Nets: Tree CNF extraction is polynomial \(\mathcal{O}(T \cdot L)\), whereas ReLU neural network minimal explanation is co-NP-complete (Katz et al., 2017).

Slide A7 addresses the critical governance, regulatory, and legal questions regarding our clinical decision-support architecture.
First, we establish a strict statutory demarcation: when an individual patient directly enters their own personal health data or uploads consumer genetic files for personal use, federal statute dictates that HIPAA does not govern that relationship. It falls under Federal Trade Commission Section 5 and the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, meaning a BAA is neither required nor legally recognized.
Second, when deployed within a healthcare provider or hospital system processing ePHI under 45 CFR Section 160.103, compliance is governed by a two-tier legal BAA execution chain: the covered entity signs BAA number 1 with our legal entity, and our legal entity executes BAA number 2 directly with AWS through AWS Artifact covering the underlying cloud infrastructure.
Third, on AWS, services are ‘HIPAA-eligible,’ not automatically compliant. We implement the full technical safeguard suite: mandatory KMS envelope encryption on S3, Firecracker microVM ephemeral compute with zero persistence in slash-tmp, strict CloudWatch log scrubbing to prevent ePHI from entering cloud telemetry, and CloudTrail audit logging under 45 CFR Section 164.312(b). Furthermore, any external AI APIs outside of AWS Bedrock would require their own signed BAA.

Mechanistic Synthesis: Claims-derived CPIC exposure signals reflect profound pharmacogenomic biological vulnerabilities. In opioid_ed, divergent CYP2D6 clearance and OPRM1 signaling drive respiratory arrest versus undertreatment-induced overdose. In ed_non_opioid, high-acuity elderly ADEs stem from dual PK/PD coagulation failure (CYP2C9/VKORC1), antiplatelet resistance (CYP2C19), and statin transport failure (SLCO1B1).

Slide A8 provides the deep mechanistic and biological defense for the specific pharmacogenomic gene markers identified across our two clinical cohorts, directly synthesizing PharmGKB Very Important Pharmacogene (VIP) pathways and CPIC Level A guidelines. In the Opioid Cohort, we focus on the pharmacokinetic anchor CYP2D6 in hepatic microsomes and the pharmacodynamic anchor OPRM1 in the central nervous system. CYP2D6 controls the rate-limiting phase I bioactivation of prodrug opioids—specifically O-demethylating codeine to morphine and tramadol to O-desmethyltramadol, which possesses over one-hundred-fold higher affinity for the mu-opioid receptor. Downstream, OPRM1 governs the Gi/Go protein-coupled receptor cascade across brainstem respiratory pacemakers like the pre-Bötzinger complex and mesolimbic reward pathways. The clinical phenotype is bidirectionally lethal: Ultra-rapid Metabolizers convert prodrugs at accelerated rates, causing a sudden toxic surge in active mu-agonists and fatal respiratory depression at standard doses; conversely, Poor Metabolizers carrying null alleles or patients with the OPRM1 118G variant experience profound analgesic failure, driving dose escalation, accidental toxicity, and ED encounters. Supporting shunt enzymes like CYP3A4 and glucuronidation clearance via UGT2B7 modulate systemic exposure. In the Polypharmacy Cohort, adverse drug events in older adults are driven by a multi-target network across hepatic clearance, transport, and coagulation. First, CYP2C19 loss-of-function star alleles (2 and 3) impair the bioactivation of clopidogrel into its active thiol metabolite, failing to inhibit platelet P2RY12 and precipitating stent thrombosis and major adverse cardiovascular events. Second, S-warfarin clearance is governed stereoselectively by hepatic CYP2C9, while its therapeutic target is VKORC1 in the endoplasmic reticulum; patients combining CYP2C9 reduced-function variants with the VKORC1 -1639A low-expression promoter variant experience severe clearance failure and extreme target sensitivity, driving supratherapeutic INR and catastrophic hemorrhage. Finally, SLCO1B1 encodes the OATP1B1 sinusoidal influx transporter; the star 5 variant halts hepatic statin uptake, trapping statins in systemic circulation where they reach toxic skeletal muscle concentrations, triggering severe statin-induced myopathy and rhabdomyolysis.
