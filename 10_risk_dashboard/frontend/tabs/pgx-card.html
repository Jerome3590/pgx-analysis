1. Gene data

AncestryDNA, 23andMe, and MyHeritage files are genealogy arrays. They show which variants were typed. They do not show which chromosome copy each variant is on, and they do not count extra or missing gene copies. A prescribing claim needs both, so this card does not assign a diplotype, a metabolizer status, or a dose from those files. A missing site is Data Not Present in File, not a normal allele. A lab line such as CYP2C19,*1,*2 still uses the official diplotype table.

Important medical and technical notice

For informational and exploratory purposes only. This tool does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or clinical pharmacogenomic recommendations.

  1. Unphased haplotypes. Consumer array files do not determine whether variants sit on the same chromosome or opposite chromosomes, so a CPIC star-allele diplotype is not assigned.
  2. Missing copy number. Microarray files cannot count gene copies. CYP2D6 duplications and deletions are not measured.
  3. Incomplete coverage. A site that is not in the file is marked Data Not Present in File. It is not treated as a normal allele.

Do not change medications from this report. Confirm any finding with a CLIA-certified, CAP-accredited pharmacogenomic test ordered by a licensed clinician.

Format: Gene,Variant1,Variant2. Example: CYP2D6,*1,*4
AncestryDNA, 23andMe, MyHeritage, Excel, or VCF. Parsed locally. Raw text is not stored. Coverage is the share of official CPIC sites present in the file.
