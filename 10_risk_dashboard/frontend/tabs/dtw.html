High-Risk vs Low-Risk Trajectories (drugs)

Research question: Do patients who experience adverse outcomes follow different drug sequences than those who do not? Compares trajectory cluster membership across outcome groups (target=1 vs target=0) to identify which drug-sequence archetypes are enriched for ED visits or polypharmacy events. Times Between Sequences (N3)

Research question: Does faster drug cycling (shorter gaps between prescriptions) associate with adverse outcomes? N3 = mean days between consecutive pharmacy/medical events per patient. Left panel: mean inter-event gap by routine vs utilization category. Right panel: mean days from first event to target outcome, revealing how long before an adverse event the drug activity pattern begins.

Target Pathway Patterns (drugs)

Ranks drug codes by appearance rate among target=1 trajectories only, highlighting the most common pre-event medications. Common Sequences Heatmap (Drug / ICD / CPT)

Research question: Are there stereotyped orderings — codes that almost always appear early vs. late in patient trajectories? Rows = individual drug/ICD/CPT codes; columns = position in sequence (1st event, 2nd, …). Darker cells = higher frequency at that position. Use the activity-type selector to switch between Drug, ICD, and CPT views. Consistent dark diagonals or clusters indicate fixed-order pathways.

Routine vs Utilization (Outcomes)

Research question: Do patients who receive routine preventive screenings (administrative ICD codes) have lower adverse outcome rates than those who do not? Compares outcome rate between patients with ≥1 routine admin ICD event vs 0 routine events. Sub-panels show mean medical/prescription event counts by group and outcome rate stratified by total utilization level (low vs extreme), testing whether routine care modifies the relationship between high utilization and adverse outcomes. Data: full 2016–2019 pipeline.

Time Between Events Histogram

Research question: What is the typical gap between consecutive medical events in this cohort? The N3 feature (mean inter-event interval) is a key temporal predictor in the risk model. This distribution directly informs the model's temporal feature and the DTW trajectory clustering above.

Bin Transition Matrix (year-over-year patient movement)

Research question: How many patients moved between event-density bins across consecutive training years (2016–2019)? Each cell shows the count of patients who were in bin From in year Y and bin To in year Y+1. Diagonal = stable; upper-right = escalation; lower-left = de-escalation. Patient Flow Across Bins by Year (Sankey)

Research question: What are the dominant patient migration paths through event-density bins over the training window? Each column = one training year; flows show patient volume moving between bins year-over-year. Wide flows = large patient populations; thin flows = rare transitions. Escalating flows (low→extreme) indicate worsening utilization trajectories.

Per-Year Bin Distribution

Research question: Is the event-density distribution of this cohort shifting over time? Each bar = one training year; stacked segments = n patients per bin. An increasing extreme segment year-over-year suggests rising polypharmacy or adverse-event risk in this population.

Escalation / De-escalation Rates

Summary rates across all consecutive year pairs. Escalation = patient moved to a higher density bin; de-escalation = moved to lower bin; stable = same bin. High escalation rates suggest a cohort whose drug utilization is intensifying over time.
