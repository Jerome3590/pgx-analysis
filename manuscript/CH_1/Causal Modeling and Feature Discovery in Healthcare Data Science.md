**Chapter 1: Systematic Literature Review of Causal-Oriented Modeling and Feature Discovery in Healthcare**  
**1\. Introduction**This chapter sets out to review the existing literature on causal-oriented modeling, noise reduction, and feature discovery in large-scale healthcare data 1\. Adapting the framework of a systematic quantitative literature review—a method proven effective in mapping complex, multi-disciplinary knowledge landscapes by charting what is known and unknown 2—this review evaluates current machine learning applications in healthcare. The primary objective is to understand the state of binary classification models that utilize drug exposures, ICD/CPT codes, and classification outcomes 1, 3, establishing the foundation for robust predictive modeling.  
**2\. Review Methodology**Following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) guidelines, this review systematically extracts and evaluates relevant academic literature 4\. A systematic quantitative approach is utilized to minimize subjective bias by charting the trajectory of health data science models and quantifying the usage of specific algorithms, variables, and architectural choices 2, 5\. Search terms focus on the integration of All-Payer Claims Database (APCD) inputs 6, pre-treatment covariates 3, and advanced feature screening methodologies.  
**3\. Technological Context and Predictive Modeling**The review synthesizes the theoretical underpinnings and current applications of key technologies in healthcare predictive modeling:

* **Gradient Boosting and Ensemble Methods:** A significant focus is placed on the use of CatBoost, XGBoost, and XGBoost RF mode 7, 8\. The literature evaluates how CatBoost handles categorical features natively, while XGBoost and its Random Forest (RF) mode provide a robustness benchmark to test ensemble effects 9\. The use of Monte Carlo cross-validation (MC-CV) for aggregating feature importance scores across models and folds is reviewed as a critical consensus approach for robust feature selection and model agreement 7, 9, 10\.  
* **Pattern and Process Mining:** The integration of process mining tools to derive stable feature sets and visual analytics is a growing area of interest. The review examines the application of BupaR, Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), and FP-Growth 1, 11, 12\. Specifically, BupaR is highlighted for its utility in post-target exploratory data analysis (EDA) to detect target leakage and refine feature importance before final model training 7, 10\.  
* **Clinical Interpretability (SHAP and FFA):** To bridge the gap between complex algorithms and clinical usability, the review assesses the combined use of global and local SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values to quantify feature impacts 8, 13\. This is reviewed alongside rule-based Formal Feature Attribution (FFA), which utilizes SHAP-filtered selection to generate prioritized, interpretable explanation rules 11, 14\.  
* **Pharmacogenomics (PGx):** The literature surrounding pharmacogenomics is reviewed to understand the role of PGx derivatives and genetic-drug interaction modeling in secondary feature engineering 8, 12, 15\.

**4\. Identified Gaps and Architectural Innovations**The systematic review identifies several critical gaps in current large-scale data workflows, particularly concerning noise reduction, post-event data leakage, and protocol violations 15, 16\. Current literature often lacks frameworks for rigorous temporal validation 9, 17\. Consequently, the review establishes the necessity for advanced system architectures, such as a partition-first approach that utilizes S3-backed checkpoints and DuckDB for parallelizable, efficient SQL-based transformations 12\. Furthermore, it highlights the need for strict temporal holdout strategies—such as training models on 2016–2018 data, testing on a 2019 holdout, and actively excluding 2020 data to prevent COVID-19 healthcare disruption leakage 9, 17\.
